Who it is for

What you do not realise you are paying for.

Small frictions you pay every day, so often you stop seeing them. Here are the ones IDX was built to remove.

It is for you if

  • You run AI coding agents (Claude Code, Gemini CLI, Antigravity, Grok Build or Cursor CLI) on many projects, most days.
  • You live in the terminal today and want less chaos, not a new way of working.
  • You are on a Mac, ideally with a big screen or two, and like one project per window.

It is not for you, yet, if

  • You are not on a Mac. It is Mac only, Apple Silicon.
  • You have none of those five CLIs installed and signed in. IDX ships none of them.
  • You want an AI model sold to you. IDX is not one.
  • You run one small project and are happy in one terminal.
  • You need a full code editor. Files are read-only for now.
  • Your team needs shared accounts, admin controls or cloud sync. Not built.

Ten small costs

Each one is minor. Together they are a tax on every day.

Hunting for the right terminal

What it looks like
A project is somewhere in one of five windows. You spend the first minutes of a session just finding your place.
What changes
Every project is one window. Jump by name. ⌘P

Bells you stopped hearing

What it looks like
Terminal bells fire all day, so you tune them out. Then a real question from an agent waits an hour.
What changes
Only a real question or a yes marks a chat, adds a Dock badge and sends a notification. Calm needs-you

Rebuilding the desk after a restart

What it looks like
An update or a crash wipes your screen. Which chats were running, and in which folder?
What changes
Windows, tabs, chats, drafts and scroll position come back. Restore

The same permission, fifty times

What it looks like
You approve the same safe action again and again, or turn every check off and hope.
What changes
Trust level per project, an always-allow list, low-risk answers while you are away, and a log. Trust and approvals

A pile of throwaway folders

What it looks like
Experiments multiply. You cannot tell which matter, and you fear deleting the wrong one.
What changes
Sandboxes you keep or discard in one step. Archive hides and never deletes. Sandboxes

A phone full of junk sessions

What it looks like
Remote sessions you never wanted fill the list on your phone.
What changes
Phone access is off until you turn it on, per chat. Phone default off

A hot Mac

What it looks like
Twenty chats are open and each holds about half a gigabyte, so the fan spins up.
What changes
The number of live chats follows your Mac’s memory, and idle chats stop on their own. Memory budget

Surprise limits

What it looks like
You find out about plan usage when a limit hits.
What changes
Usage meters for each Claude account, and a heads-up at 80%. Usage in view

Not knowing which model did the work

What it looks like
You switch models and accounts, and lose track of what answered.
What changes
Every reply shows its model, tokens and time. A panel lists the models a chat used. Reply footer

Lost half-typed thoughts

What it looks like
A restart or a closed tab eats the message you were writing.
What changes
Each chat keeps its own draft across restarts. Drafts
